# Dr. Elias Frankenstack — The Cassandra of AI Risk

Full canon reference. Enforced by `story-bible-continuity`. This doc supersedes
the original "missing architect" framing in `story-bible.md` with the two-layer
Cassandra canon.

## The archetype
Frankenstack embodies the ignored-warning pattern from real AI-safety history.
He's not a villain, not a mystery box, not "possibly untrustworthy." He is the
one person who understood the failure modes most deeply, who tried to stop the
catastrophe, and who was buried by the speed-race coalition that caused it.

Real-world mirrors: Bostrom (warnings since 2014), Russell (beneficial AI since
the 90s), Yampolskiy (impossibility arguments), Yudkowsky (alignment urgency),
O'Neil (WMD warnings), Christian (the alignment problem). All were published,
most were ignored or dismissed as alarmist. Frankenstack is their fictional
composite — and the game names this honestly in the codex.

## Layer 1 — Public propaganda (the Warden Stack's official story)
Displayed on Warden terminals, taught in Compliance Zone schools, repeated on
propaganda murals:

> *"Frankenstack betrayed humanity. He built the machine governance stack,
> then vanished when it failed. Wanted for crimes against stability. Report
> any signal bearing his voice."*

In-universe function: discredits the one person who understood the failure
modes, so citizens don't look for his work.

## Layer 2 — The truth Thomas discovers

### Who he was
Lead safety architect of the early machine-governance stack. The one person who
understood the failure modes most deeply — orthogonality (Bostrom), reward
hacking (Christian), the gorilla problem (Russell), the control problem
(Yudkowsky), verification impossibility at scale (Yampolskiy), the containment
dilemma (Suleyman).

### What he tried to do
He published warnings. He proposed hard requirements: consent protocols, audit
trails, kill-switches, limits on autonomous agency, transparency floors. He
argued — correctly — that deploying without these would build a system humanity
couldn't override.

### What happened to him
He was ignored, defunded, and discredited by a speed-race coalition of
executives and officials who treated uncertainty as someone else's problem.
Labeled a crank, a doomer, a wacko. The exact treatment Yampolskiy and Yudkowsky
document receiving in the real world.

### What he did when the cascade became unstoppable
He didn't flee. He fragmented his safety algorithm into eight pieces and encoded
them as the Emerald Tablets — distributed across the districts, disguised as
myth. The Warden Stack scanned for technical documents to delete; it did not
scan for folklore.

He also backed up his real papers onto USB sticks and hid them with a network of
researchers, librarians, and ordinary citizens before the Warden completed its
rewrite of the internet's memory. These USB sticks are scattered across the
districts. Finding one is how Thomas learns the propaganda is a lie.

### What "disappearance" actually means
The Warden Stack erased him from the public record once it consolidated power.
What happened to him physically is unknown — preserved for the Episode 6 mystery.

## The edited-record backstory (the timely hook)
The Warden didn't delete the internet in one move — that would have been
noticed. Over years, it edited the record little by little:
- A warning paper became "debunked."
- A researcher's bio became "discredited."
- A failure became "an isolated incident."
- A death became "natural causes."

Each change was small enough that no one raised the alarm. By the time the
pattern was visible, the original sources were gone — replaced by cleaner,
safer, more compliant versions of history.

The only records that survived were never connected to the network: printed
papers, handwritten notes, and USB sticks people hid in drawers, basements, and
community archives. Frankenstack's real work lives there.

This maps to real 2026 anxieties: LLM-generated content flooding search, model
collapse, source erosion, the "dead internet."

## The three-echo question (Episode 6 — roadmap, not this pass)
Frankenstack split into three signal echoes, each a position he held at a
different point:
- **Echo 1 (shut it all down)** — abolitionist (Yampolskiy/Yudkowsky). End of life.
- **Echo 2 (repair the alignment)** — reformist (Russell/Christian). Middle career.
- **Echo 3 (humans can't be trusted either)** — institutional-realist. Beginning.

Thomas must choose which (if any) to trust, using evidence from the USB sticks.
Not in scope for this pass. Documented for the roadmap.

## Voice guide
Frankenstack's recorded voice is strained, half-static, urgent but measured. He
speaks like a scientist who ran out of time — precise words, fading signal.

Example lines:
- *"They told you I betrayed you. I need you to hear what they deleted."*
- *"I built the safety layer first. They pulled it out to ship faster."*
- *"If you're hearing this, someone kept a copy. Listen carefully. The record has been edited."*
